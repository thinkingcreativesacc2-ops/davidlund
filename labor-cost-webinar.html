The Reality Check

Common Labor Cost Mistakes Hotel Leaders Make

Most of these mistakes aren't caused by bad leadership — they're caused by a system that never taught hotel leaders how to see labor clearly.

01

Scheduling to Yesterday's Numbers

Building schedules around last week's occupancy instead of tomorrow's actual demand — and paying for it in overtime or missed service.

02

Chasing One Number

Treating labor cost percentage as the only metric that matters, while ignoring productivity, guest scores, and turnover it's masking.

03

Reactive, Not Strategic, Cuts

Slashing hours the moment a budget flags red, instead of forecasting and adjusting before the number ever gets there.

04

Disconnecting Labor From Guest Experience

Making staffing decisions in isolation from the guest satisfaction and review scores those decisions directly influence.

05

Leaving Department Heads Out

Keeping payroll accountability at the GM level instead of teaching every department head to own their own labor line.

06

Reading the P&L Instead of Forecasting It

Finding out about a labor problem after it's already on the financial statement, rather than seeing it coming weeks in advance.
